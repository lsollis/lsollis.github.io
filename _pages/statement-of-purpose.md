*ICS Ph.D. portfolio, category 1.*

## Research Interests

My research focuses on understanding when, why, and for whom medical AI systems fail, particularly when applied to populations and clinical settings that differ from those in which they were developed. I am interested in identifying the sources of these performance gaps and developing methods that make medical AI more reliable and accessible to the patients who need it.

This interest connects my work across several areas of medical screening. With Dr. Peter Washington, I evaluated how autism screening models trained on cohorts from New Zealand and Saudi Arabia generalized to clinically diagnosed children in Poland. I also contributed to research on remote Parkinson's disease assessment, where our group investigated how input devices and handedness influence model performance. With Dr. John Shepherd at the University of Hawaiʻi Cancer Center, I examined demographic representation and implementation challenges in AI for skin cancer screening. More recently, I evaluated federated learning for mammographic cancer detection across three institutions, including a diverse Pacific Island cohort. We found that conventional federated strategies primarily benefited the largest institution, while a personalized approach improved performance at the worst-performing site. Across these projects, I have become increasingly interested in the limitations of aggregate performance metrics and the importance of understanding how models behave for different patient populations.

Looking ahead, I am particularly interested in two related research directions.

**Breast imaging for women with implants and other challenging imaging characteristics.** Breast implants can obscure tissue during mammography, reducing cancer detection sensitivity even when additional implant-displaced views are obtained. Yet much of the foundational evidence quantifying these effects predates modern digital mammography, and women with implants have frequently been excluded from the development and evaluation of mammographic AI. This raises important questions about how implants affect model performance and whether existing AI systems can reliably evaluate these examinations. I want to investigate these questions and develop approaches that better accommodate the imaging characteristics of augmented and reconstructed breasts. This interest is also personal, as several women in my family have breast implants, including following breast cancer treatment. More broadly, I am interested in extending this work to patients with other implanted devices, post-surgical changes, or anatomical characteristics that challenge conventional imaging models.

**Federated learning for rare diseases and underrepresented populations.** Many clinically important populations are too small or geographically dispersed for any single institution to assemble sufficient training data. Federated learning offers a way to collaborate across institutions without transferring patient data, but conventional approaches do not necessarily benefit all participants equally. My previous work demonstrated how differences among institutions can produce unequal outcomes even when overall model performance improves. I want to investigate how population and institutional heterogeneity influence these results and develop personalized federated methods that support reliable modeling for rare conditions and populations with limited data. I am especially interested in approaches that allow smaller institutions to benefit from collaborative learning without sacrificing performance for their own patients.

These interests reflect a broader research goal: to move beyond demonstrating that medical AI works on average and toward understanding when it can be trusted, when it needs to be adapted, and when a different approach is required.

## Professional Goals

My long-term goal is to contribute to the development of medical AI systems that are both methodologically rigorous and useful in clinical practice. Following my Ph.D., I intend to pursue a postdoctoral position that provides specialized training in federated learning, medical imaging, or methods for improving model reliability in challenging and underrepresented populations. I want to deepen my methodological expertise while working with researchers and clinical collaborators who are developing solutions to these problems.

Beyond postdoctoral training, I am open to pursuing research in industry, academia, or a combination of both. My priority is to work in an environment where I can make meaningful contributions to the development, validation, and implementation of medical AI. Before beginning doctoral study, I worked in technical and operational roles where the reliability of systems had direct consequences for the people who depended on them. That experience continues to shape my research interests and my desire to see advances in machine learning translate into practical improvements in patient care.

In industry, I would welcome opportunities to develop and evaluate medical AI systems at scale, gaining experience with the challenges of clinical deployment and ongoing model monitoring. In academia, I would be interested in leading an interdisciplinary research group that works closely with clinical partners to address persistent gaps in medical AI performance and accessibility. I see value in both paths and expect my experiences and opportunities following postdoctoral training to help shape that decision.

## Teaching and Mentoring

Teaching is also an important part of my professional interests. Although I have not yet had the opportunity to teach in a university classroom, I discovered how much I enjoy teaching while serving as an Army officer, where I taught introductory IT and command-line skills to soldiers with varied technical backgrounds. I continue to find that same satisfaction in coaching my son's youth soccer team. I would welcome the opportunity to teach computer science at the university level, helping students develop confidence with challenging technical material and mentoring those entering the field from nontraditional backgrounds.

Ultimately, whether my career takes me into industry, academia, or both, I want my work to help close the gap between what medical AI can achieve under ideal conditions and what it delivers for patients in the real world.
